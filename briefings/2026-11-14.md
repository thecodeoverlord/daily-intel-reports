## GEOPOLITICS

### 1. Trump, Xi and Putin to hold first trilateral summit — Shenzhen, 19 Nov
**Confidence: HIGH on the announcement · LOW on substance**

The White House confirmed Friday that the three leaders will meet on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen — the first gathering of the heads of the world's three largest nuclear powers. APEC runs 18–19 November; the trilateral is set for the Thursday.

Analysts quoted by AP expect a symbolic event rather than a breakthrough. The value is procedural: it creates a single venue where the Ukraine file, Taiwan, tariffs and Iran can be traded, and it gives all three leaders a domestic win without requiring concessions.

**Why it matters:** if the trilateral produces a framework — even a vague one — it will be presented as the umbrella under which the Ukraine talks continue. If it produces nothing, expect the Kremlin to blame European intransigence.

### 2. Kremlin tables maximalist European security demand; EU calls it a "trap"
**Confidence: HIGH on positions · MED on trajectory**

After 4.5 hours with Witkoff and Kushner in Moscow on 12 November, the Kremlin said it wants "a new, long-term architecture of indivisible security and peace." Translated: the revival of the December 2021 draft treaties — a permanent bar on NATO membership for Ukraine and Georgia, plus rollback of NATO infrastructure from post-1991 members.

EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas called it a trap designed to shift attention away from a ceasefire, and set out the EU's sequencing: ceasefire → peace agreement → security architecture. The Kremlin then walked European participation back: Peskov said no final decision until Moscow judges Europe's "true intentions." Putin warned that Western troops in Ukraine after a deal would restart the war.

**Why it matters:** "Europe is in the room" is a US claim, not a settled fact. Treat any reporting that presents it as agreed with suspicion.

- BBC: [Uncompromising Putin ups demands for peace with Ukraine](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cq39glwprknjo)

### 3. EU hardens its line and starts counting the money
**Confidence: HIGH**

At the European Political Community summit in Dublin (12 Nov) and the informal European Council (13 Nov), EU leaders fixed three principles: Ukraine chooses its own alliances; European security cannot be discussed without Europeans; the existing European security architecture does not need reinventing.

The financing problem is now explicit. EU officials estimate a **€70bn gap for 2027** on top of the €90bn Ukraine Support Loan — €40bn defence, €30bn budget. German Chancellor Merz has asked the EU to find an additional **$77bn** for Kyiv next year and called it an "essential prerequisite" for approving the next EU budget. Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Bulgaria have already opposed linking budget approval to new Ukraine funding.

Calendar: Foreign Affairs Council 16 Nov, FAC Defence 17 Nov, European Council 26–27 Nov (Ukraine financing plus the 22nd sanctions package), EU–UK summit 20 Nov.

### 1. Pokrovsk remains the schwerpunkt: 243 engagements in 24 hours
**Confidence: MED** — single source (Ukrainian General Staff), routine reporting

Ukraine's General Staff reported 243 combat engagements on 13 November, 47 of them in the Pokrovsk sector and 10 in Kostiantynivka. Russia flew 44 air strikes, dropped 155 guided bombs, employed 3,023 one-way attack drones and shelled 1,775 settlements and positions.

### 2. Hormuz: IRGC claims two tankers struck mines, including a supertanker
**Confidence: HIGH that the claim was made · MED that mines are the cause**

The Revolutionary Guards' navy said two oil tankers that had switched off radar and positioning systems, attempting an unauthorised transit, "exploded and caught fire after striking a naval mine." One was a supertanker. No names or flags released.

This is not isolated. It follows a third tanker on 10 November and two on 7 November. Since the war began in February, Iran has required its authorisation to transit and bans Israel- and US-affiliated shipping. The US has imposed a naval blockade on Iranian ports and provides military support to compliant tankers — and US military statements say the main routes have been cleared of mines, which directly contradicts the IRGC account.

**Assessment:** the IRGC statement is corroborated across outlets but all trace to the IRGC. Iran International notes it could not be independently verified. Treat the attributions as contested and the campaign as real.

## ECONOMY

### 1. IEA: the oil market is back in surplus
**Confidence: HIGH** — primary source

The November Oil Market Report is the most important economic document of the day. Key findings:

- Global supply rose 2.3 mb/d in October to **105.4 mb/d**; Q4 supply now exceeds forecast demand by 1.7 mb/d, the first surplus since the war began.
- Observed inventories **rose 52 mb** in October — the first build since June — though onshore stocks remain at their lowest since at least 2016. Cumulative draw since the war started: **462 mb**.
- Brent fell from a **$136** high in early October to **$93** at time of writing.
- 2026 demand: **−2.7 mb/d** to 102.3 mb/d; 2027: **+2 mb/d**. Supply rebounds 6.6 mb/d in 2027 to 108.1 mb/d.
- Iran's 2027 outlook was revised down because of the US blockade.
- A further **71 mb** of emergency reserves will be released by end-February, including **15 mb of diesel**.

The diesel number is the one to watch: at close to **$180/bbl**, it is still ~77% above pre-war. Product tightness, not crude, is the binding constraint.

**Confidence: HIGH on data and pricing · MED on the path**

## CYBER / SPACE / CRITICAL INFRASTRUCTURE

### 1. Starshield throttling is now failing 100% of Ukrainian interceptor missions
**Confidence: HIGH that the disruption is real and disputed · MED on cause and attribution**

Ukraine says Starshield — the military-grade Starlink variant, built for US government use and operated by a commercial contractor — is imposing a **161 km/h (100 mph)** speed ceiling across the entire front line and occupied territory. Interceptor drones chasing jet-powered Shaheds cannot reach the threshold. A Ukrainian official told Axios the failure rate went 10% → 50% → 100% over three days, coinciding with the start of US–Russia talks in Abu Dhabi.

SpaceX says: "This is false. There has been no change." The Pentagon has not explained. The European Commission called the reports "deeply concerning" and "unacceptable" if confirmed, and demanded urgent restoration. Senators Shaheen and Tillis demanded a full accounting; a previous bipartisan request for an inspector-general investigation was declined.

This is the **second episode**. The first ran 14–24 October; the WSJ reported Musk ordered it and that Trump asked him to reverse it. Ukraine's access contract expires mid-2027 and there is no effective alternative to LEO satcom.

**Why it matters:** this is the clearest case yet of a single commercial provider holding veto power over a belligerent's tactical air defence. It also raises a harder question — whether the throttling is commercial policy, US government leverage, or both.

### 2. UK moves to jail undersea cable saboteurs for up to 14 years
**Confidence: HIGH**

The UK is drafting legislation to replace an 1885 statute with penalties of up to 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines for deliberately damaging subsea cables and pipelines. Context: subsea fibre carries ~99% of intercontinental internet traffic; 150–200 faults occur worldwide each year, 70–80% from fishing and anchor drag.

Industry's objection is structural, not legal: new cables are laid along existing corridors and share landing points — "fake diversity" — so a single incident can still take out multiple routes.

## SOURCING NOTE

**Method.** 13 semantic web searches via Exa, plus direct retrieval of primary documents. Search was the backbone of this edition because it reached non-anglophone and specialist sources that RSS alone did not: Xinhua, Anadolu, Nikkei, NHK, SCMP, Kyiv Post, Ukrainska Pravda, Dabanga and ISPI.

**Primary sources used directly:** IEA Oil Market Report (Nov 2026), OPEC MOMR, EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook, Federal Reserve FOMC statement and chair's transcript, European Council press releases and forward look.

**Grading rules.**
- **HIGH** — primary document, or corroborated across multiple independent outlets with named attribution.
- **MED** — single reputable outlet, anonymous officials, belligerent-sourced claims, or figures from a party to the conflict.
- **LOW** — single-source, unverified, or materially contested.

**Known gaps.**
- No Chinese-language or Russian-language primary source was consulted. Both would be needed to grade the trilateral and the Kremlin's demands above MED.
- Hormuz mine attribution remains contested: IRGC claims versus CENTCOM statements. Not resolved.
- Gaza casualty figures are Health Ministry figures and should be treated as such.
- Sudan casualty and displacement figures are UN estimates with wide error bars.
- The Microsoft outage and the FAA ground stop are reported separately; no link between them has been established and none is asserted here.

**Corrections.** This edition supersedes the 14 November test run, which understated the Hormuz incident (two tankers, one a supertanker, plus a sustained campaign) and flattened a genuine US–Russia disagreement over European participation in the Ukraine talks into false consensus.
